What it is

A money manager's routine, done by a team of AI helpers.

A family or charity has savings in company shares and rules like "exactly 8 companies", "at most 20% in one", "no oil and gas". Every few months someone must read reports, choose what to buy and sell, check each rule and explain it. This project does that, shows every reason, and lets a person decide.

The team

Agents, tools, and how they talk.

A supervisor decides who works next. Two agents run as separate services, as if they belonged to other teams, and receive tasks over A2A (agent-to-agent). Tools are shared through MCP, a standard toolbox where each agent may only use what its job needs.

Hybrid search (RAG)

It looks things up before it answers.

The research agent only answers from passages it found in real reports, and cites them. It searches two ways at once, merges the results, and a small AI "reranker" puts the best passages first.

Quantum, simply

Each stock is a yes or no.

Choosing 10 of 20 stocks has 184,756 combinations, and the number explodes as problems grow. A qubit is a natural yes/no unit, so each stock gets one qubit. The quantum algorithm QAOA treats all combinations together and makes the good ones more likely. Click the squares to build a portfolio of exactly 10.

How it is used

One qubit per stock (17-20 qubits). The circuit can only produce valid answers (exactly the right number of stocks). It runs on a quantum simulator (Qiskit) on a laptop.

How it is checked

A normal computer tries every combination, so the quantum answer is graded against the proven best. A "hybrid" step then polishes it with small swaps.

What we found

At the default setting quantum found the proven best portfolio for 3 of the 4 clients and came within about 10% for the Indian client; the hybrid polish step reached the best every time. At this size the normal computer is faster and exact, so no "quantum advantage" is claimed. The value is a fair, working integration, ready for bigger problems.

Human in the loop

You decide. It learns.

Nothing is traded until a person approves. If you change something, the agents re-plan at once, and (if you tick "remember") follow your preference for this client from then on.

✓ Approve✎ Change✕ Reject
1

Plan

The agents propose what to buy and sell.

2

You review

Remove a stock, set a smaller maximum per stock, or ask for less risk, and say why.

3

Re-plan

The plan is rebuilt with your change and checked again, then you decide again.

4

Remember

Your preference is saved for this client. Next time the first plan already follows it, and says so.

5

Measure

Approvals and changes are tracked in Langfuse: fewer changes over time means the loop is working.

Preferences only make the rules stricter, never looser, and can be removed at any time.
